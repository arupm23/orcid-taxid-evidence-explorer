Basic details

Evidence, not an automated verdict.

The MVP validates an ORCID iD, retrieves linked PubMed publications, follows NCBI nucleotide-record links, resolves canonical TAXIDs, and presents cited relationships for qualified human review.

Provenance first

Every researcher–organism relationship is connected to public publication and NCBI evidence.

No generative LLM

Deterministic templates present retrieved facts without generating or modifying scientific claims.

Human authority

The tool does not approve orders, assign trust scores, or determine legitimate intent.

Motivation

Reduce screening friction.

When a DNA synthesis order involves an organism or biological function of concern, reviewers may need to investigate whether the customer has a credible scientific reason for requesting it. Relevant information is distributed across researcher profiles, publications, and biological databases.

Manual investigation can be slow, inconsistent, and difficult to audit. It can also delay legitimate work by vaccine researchers, diagnostic developers, public-health scientists, and other responsible customers. This project explores whether public scholarly identifiers can make that evidence-gathering step faster and more transparent.

Positive impact

Better-informed human review.

  • Reduce repetitive literature and database searches.
  • Provide citations for researcher–organism relationships.
  • Make screening investigations more consistent and auditable.
  • Reduce avoidable delays for legitimate scientific work.
  • Help reviewers focus on unexplained or concerning requests.
  • Complement existing customer and sequence-screening systems.

Responsible use

Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

Publication history does not prove identity, authorization, laboratory experience, biosafety approval, benign intent, or legitimate end use. ORCID and PubMed coverage are incomplete. This project should be treated as one evidence source within a broader screening process.
